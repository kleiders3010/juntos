# Juntos: free video calls for everyone

Video calls in the browser, on phones (iPhone and Android) and computers. No account, no time limit, nothing to
install, open source.

**Use it:** https://meet.jurisgames.com (or juntos.jurisgames.com) · **Android app:** [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.jurisgames.juntos) or [Juntos.apk](https://github.com/kleiders3010/juntos/releases/latest) ·
[Privacy](https://meet.jurisgames.com/privacy)

- **Start a call** and send the link. People type their name and join.
- Schedule a call for later, like Meet's and Zoom's: title, day, time, how long, repeat (every day, weekday, week, 2 weeks, month). Copy the link or the invitation, add it to Google Calendar or Outlook.com, or save a calendar file (.ics, with your time zone, so a repeating call stays at the same time after a clock change) for Apple Calendar, Outlook and others. The calls you planned are listed on the start page of that device, and you are their host. A QR code opens the link on a phone, also from Invite people during a call.
- Works on weak connections, like Meet's data saver and Zoom's connection bars: bars on each picture show how good each connection is (tap them for the delay and lost packets); each picture only gets the video size it needs (a small picture gets a small video, a hidden one none) and sound always goes first; Video quality: Auto, Data saver (only the main picture, yours at 360p; on by default on mobile data) or Audio only; when your connection gets weak the call shows less video by itself, then pauses video so the sound keeps going, says so at the top, and comes back when the connection does. Tap a paused picture to see it.
- Background blur or a picture behind you (runs on your own device).
- Adjust video lighting, like Meet's: when you look dark (a dim room, a window behind you) Juntos suggests it, and it brightens you gently as the light changes (on your own device, under any background).
- Chat, screen sharing, reactions. In chat, like Meet's: react to a message with an emoji (your most used ones first; tap one to add yours or take it back, point at it to see who) and quote a message in your reply; pin your own message to the top of the chat for everyone, people who join later too (hosts can unpin anyone's; `python worker/chatpin_test.py`). Raise a hand, like Meet's: People lists raised hands in the order they went up, hosts lower one or all, and a hand goes down by itself once you speak.
- Open someone's shared screen in a window of its own (on a computer), like Meet's: put it on another screen or size it as you like; close it to bring it back.
- Up to 16 people in a call (tested with 16 people at once). Everyone is always heard; in a big call only the videos of the people who spoke last are shown (up to 9, like Zoom's active speakers), the others show their name and a tap shows their video.
- Layouts, like Meet's: Auto, Tiled, Spotlight (only who's talking) and Sidebar (who's talking big, the others beside); pin people (up to 6, they share the big space; hosts can pin up to 3 for everyone); hide people with their camera off; your own picture with everyone, small in a corner or hidden. Remembered on your device.
- Whoever starts the call is its host, shown to everyone in People. Like Meet and Zoom: make co-hosts (the same controls, except making co-hosts or removing the host); let someone speak or not ("Allow to speak" / "Don't allow to speak": a host never turns anyone's microphone on or off, and who isn't allowed raises a hand to ask), or nobody at once; choose what everyone can do (speak, share their screen, send chat messages, turn on captions); remove someone; lock the call so newcomers ask to join and a host lets them in (one by one, or everyone waiting at once). The room server keeps the roles and permissions, and every device holds to them, whatever another device sends. If the hosts leave, whoever is waiting comes in.
- Who can join, like Zoom's passcode and waiting room: when starting or scheduling a call, choose "people ask to join" and / or a passcode (4 to 10 letters or numbers, a 6-digit one is ready). The invite link carries the passcode (after "#", never sent to a server), so people with the link just join; others type it. The host can change or remove it during the call. Only a one-way hash of it is kept, and wrong guesses wait a minute after 5 tries.
- A shared whiteboard with no edges: pen in three sizes, rectangles, circles, arrows, text, an eraser and undo; move and zoom your own view (pinch or Ctrl + scroll to zoom; two fingers, scrolling or the hand to move); make it big with everyone small floating over it.
- Sound: check your microphone and speaker before joining (a live level, a test sound, record a few seconds and hear them back); background noise reduction (on, can be turned off); a note when you talk with the microphone off; hold Space to talk while muted.
- Live captions, for people who can't hear: big white-on-black text with the speaker's name, three sizes, remembered. Each person's own browser turns their own voice into text, and only while someone has captions on and the microphone is on. A browser that can't (Firefox, some phones) gets help: the browser of someone in the call that can listen to a received voice (Chrome or Edge on a computer) makes that person's captions, and both are told. Translated captions, like Meet's (a paid feature there): pick a language and the others' captions show in it, translated by your own browser on your device (Chrome and Edge on computers; a language that isn't on the device yet downloads after a click). A browser with no translator asks one in the call that has it. The .txt keeps what was said and the translation under it. Save everything said as a .txt, also after leaving.
- Switch device, like Meet's "Switch here": move the call from your computer to your phone (or back) with a link or a QR code; on the other device pick "Switch here" (this one leaves, and a host stays a host) or "Join here too".
- Companion mode, like Meet's: join from a second device in the same room with no microphone and no sound (so no echo), to chat, raise a hand, vote or share a screen; the others see "Name (Companion)" in People and no empty tile. "Use my audio" makes it a normal join in place.
- Attendance, like Meet's attendance report (a paid feature there): a host saves a .csv from People, or after leaving, with each person's name, first join, last exit, time in the call (added up when someone rejoins) and how many times they joined. Made on the host's device from when the host joined.
- Polls and Q&A, like Meet's: hosts start polls (or everyone, or everyone with a host approving each first); polls with up to 10 answers (responses without names, show everyone the results or only you and the host, end, delete); questions anyone can ask (anonymously if the host allows), upvote, sort by popular or recent, filter; the host marks answered, hides, deletes, turns questions off or approves each one first; hosts approve, hide, end or delete any poll. Late joiners catch up. Save it all as a .txt, also after leaving.
- Breakout rooms, like Meet's: the host splits the call into rooms (spread automatically, shuffled or moved one by one), with an optional timer; everyone is invited and joins (or stays), asks for help, returns to the main call; the host joins any room, edits rooms while open and closes them (30 seconds, then everyone is back). Sound, picture and chat stay in each room.
- Shared notes, like the notes a Zoom or Meet call can have: one text everyone writes in at the same time (nothing typed is lost when two people type at once), a meeting notes template (date, who's there, notes, action items), the host can make it "only the host can edit". Late joiners get it all. Copy it or save it as a .txt, also after leaving.
- Draw on a shared screen, like Zoom's annotation: pen, highlighter, a pen that fades by itself, arrows, boxes, text, a pointer everyone sees, an eraser, six colours, undo / redo; the marks stay on the right spot of the shared picture on every screen size. Who shares can let the others draw or not, show who drew, and erase or clear anyone's marks (so can the host); the others erase their own. Save a picture with the drawings; recordings show them. Gone when the sharing stops.
- Small window (picture-in-picture), like Meet's: keep the call over every other window, with who's talking, yourself small, captions, notices and the buttons (mic, camera, hand, captions, back to the call, leave). It can open by itself when you switch tabs. Chrome and Edge on computers; other browsers float the video of who's talking.
- Keyboard: M microphone, V camera (or Meet's Ctrl+D / Ctrl+E), H raise a hand, C chat, ? the list.
- Set how loud you hear each person, from silent to 300 % for someone quiet (only on your device, remembered).
- Record the call on your own device as an MP4 (everyone sees that you are recording). Landscape, with whoever is talking (or a shared screen) big and the others small, or everyone the same size; reactions, raised hands and chat can show in the video. No upload, no limit.
- Send feedback (start page, Devices, and after a call): a problem, an idea or something else, an optional email for an answer, and, if you keep the tick, the recent call's technical numbers (connection states, delay, lost packets, how many people; never sound, video, chat or names). "See what's sent" shows it all first. It is emailed to hello@jurisgames.com through [jurisgames-feedback](https://github.com/kleiders3010/jurisgames-feedback), which stores nothing.

## How it works
Audio, video, chat and the board go directly between the people in the call (WebRTC). A tiny Cloudflare Worker
(`worker/`) only introduces people to each other when they join a room. Per room it keeps only a hash of the host's key, whether the call is locked, a hash of its passcode if it has one, what the hosts let everyone do, and (by the random per-visit id, no names) co-hosts, speaking choices and who was let in; deleted a month after the room was last used.

No piece has to be up for a call to work; each job has a second way:
- Finding each other: public Nostr relays (`web/nsig.js`, encrypted with a key made from the call code); if none answers, the room server.
- The room server (passcodes, calls over 16 people, older calls): the Cloudflare Worker, or the same code on Node (`kinetic/`, runs on our game host at `wss://games.jurisgames.com/juntos`); each call picks one by its code and moves to the other after 6 s without an answer.
- Host controls (lock, mute, co-hosts, remove): a document signed by the host's key (`web/hostauth.js`) that every device checks, so no server has to.
- Two people who can't connect (strict routers on both sides): the Cloudflare TURN relay, or else someone in the call connected to both passes their sound and video on, with their messages sealed by a key only those two have (`web/fwd.js`); if that helper leaves, another takes over.
- The site: meet.jurisgames.com, and the same pages on GitHub Pages at https://kleiders3010.github.io/juntos/ (`tools/mirror.mjs`, built by `.github/workflows/pages.yml` on every push). Calls work across both.
- Tests that take a piece away: `worker/failover_test.py`, `worker/fwd_test.py`, `worker/serverless_test.py`, `worker/hostctl_test.py`, `worker/mirror_test.py`.

## Run locally
    cd worker && npx wrangler dev --port 8787
    # serve web/ with any static server that falls back to index.html, then open
    # http://localhost:8080/?signal=ws://127.0.0.1:8787

License: Apache-2.0. Background segmentation: MediaPipe (Apache-2.0, vendored in `web/vendor/mediapipe`, see its NOTICE.md). MP3 sound in Firefox recordings: lamejs (LGPL-3.0, vendored unchanged in `web/vendor/lamejs` with its full source `lame.all.js`; replace it with any build of lamejs).

The Worker reads one optional secret, `TURN_KEY` (a Cloudflare TURN key, set with `wrangler secret put`), for people behind strict networks; without it calls still work on most networks.
